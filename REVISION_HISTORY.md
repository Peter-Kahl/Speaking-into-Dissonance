## Corrections to Version 1.0

The following statements in Version 1.0 misdescribed their sources. They are corrected in Version 2.0, where the full text of each correction appears in Appendix A.

1. **Güngör et al. (2014).** The study compared Japanese and Turkish students, not Turkish and American adolescents.
2. **Kaplan, Gimbel and Harris (2016).** The orbitofrontal and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex were more active for challenges to non-political beliefs. Resistance to belief change was associated with the amygdala and insula.
3. **He et al. (2021).** The study does not report reduced amygdala activation in L2 reasoning. It reports dorsolateral prefrontal involvement in loss aversion in a gambling task.
4. **Miller et al. (2025).** The comparison groups were described inconsistently, and a trait conclusion was drawn from a state finding. Proficiency did not predict belief revision.
5. **Brock et al. (2025).** In the model, partial learning is not squeezed out by conformity. It remains a stable outcome and can grow with conformity.
6. **Kurnianto (2021).** The article's abstract gives 24 students and its results section 21, and the article does not frame the case in terms of conformity incentives.
7. **Festinger (1957).** The one-dollar/twenty-dollar study was reported in Festinger and Carlsmith (1959).
8. **Unsourced claims.** The claim that shame is the most common affect reported by L2 learners is withdrawn. The claim about weaker autonomic responses to taboo words in L2 is now supported by Harris, Ayçiçeği and Gleason (2003).
